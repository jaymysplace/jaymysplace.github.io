Starting a zettelkasten is hard. You mustn't paint yourself into a corner. The branches need to be broad enough to hang everything you want on them.

Scott Scheper recommends using Wikipedia's Outline of academic disciplines] as your basis, but that felt a bit too formal for me. I wanted a more chaotic, personal, organic start.

There's a Richard Feymann quote about '12 favourite problems'[1] that I probably took out of context, and used to list out the things that really are at the back of my mind all of the time, that I want to learn more about or be better at. I came up with 7 'favourite problems':

  • Being more confident
  • Being more creative
  • Creating and keeping a cosy home
  • Being more organised
  • Being more productive
  • Being a better communicator
  • Being calmer

These became my starting cards. Interestingly, once I wrote the cards and laid them out on my desk, I noticed that some were really just sub-problems of others, so I put those one level lower, giving me just 3 main branches!

My main branches are:

  1. Being more confident
  2. Being calmer
  3. Being more productive

So far, it's working. The worst I've had to do is make a sort of 'half-way point' main card, to bridge the gap between the existing stuff and the new card, and still leave room for more branches from the original card. Sometimes I have to let a topic balloon right up after giving it a passing mention in one card, but that's part of the fun, right?
