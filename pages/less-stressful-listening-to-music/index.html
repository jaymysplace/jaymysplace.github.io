I decided to unsubscribe from Apple Music a few months ago. I just didn't think I was listening to enough music to get my £10's worth[1] so I thought if I stop subscribing, then I'm allowed to continue spending up to £10 a month on buying music instead, and I'll see how I get on.

Really interestingly, I think I'm listening to a little bit more music now than when I was subscribed. I don't think it's a 'paradox of choice' thing, but more like there was some sort of pressure on me to listen when I was subscribed, like it felt like I really should be listening to something because I'm paying for it. And course once that pressure is there, all of the fun disappears. I think there might have been some element of knowing that the algorithm is watching too. Like, some sort of feeling of this listening activity having a kind of higher purpose - it's shaping what Apple Music will give me if I try to listen to an auto-playlist. Like, if I listen to the Hollow Knight soundtrack every day for weeks, actually I don't want to be shown any more Christopher Larkin stuff, it's just, like, I was loving listening to that one album for a bit. That's all there was to it.

So now that the pressure is off, I feel as though I can listen (or not listen) with no consequences, and I'm finding that I am listening to music a little more often.

I did spend £20 on a little mp3 player off Amazon and I haven't used that as much as I thought I would, but I'm fine with that, and to be honest I still don't think I've found my favourite way to listen quite yet. The mp3 player does work nicely but I think I'm really spoiled by listening on my phone and having my Airpods cut in if needed, plus, it's not a seamless move to listening in the car. But what I'm using to listen seems like the topic of a post for another time
